01 / The central thesis

Reality becomes
shareable.

Civilization is not merely a pile of inventions. It is an evolving infrastructure for preserving, transmitting, interpreting, and acting on representations of the world.

A cave painting preserves a trace of experience. Language carries memory. Mathematics expresses relationships. Law encodes rules. Archives preserve evidence. AI transforms symbolic material into new candidates for interpretation and action.

02 / Three realities

Not all realities
are the same kind.

A rigorous theory distinguishes the world being described, the symbols used to describe it, and the social arrangements that become real through shared recognition and enforcement.

01

Physical & lived reality

Material processes, ecological systems, bodies, events, and experience. Human knowledge of this reality is mediated and fallible; reality is not simply whatever we believe.

NatureBodiesEvents
02

Symbolic reality

Images, language, mathematics, maps, stories, scientific models, historical narratives, databases, and algorithms through which the world is represented.

RepresentationMeaningKnowledge
03

Institutionally constructed reality

Money, citizenship, property, borders, corporations, and legal identities. They depend on human institutions yet can have tangible consequences for freedom and survival.

RulesPowerCoordination
Critical distinction: A social reality can be constructed without being imaginary or powerless. But its historical existence does not prove that it is natural, inevitable, or morally justified.
03 / Historical arc

From visible trace
to generative systems.

A broad historical model of how humans externalize and extend knowledge. These epochs overlap; each new medium depends on earlier capacities rather than simply replacing them.

01

The visible trace

Cave artMarks

Images and material traces preserve aspects of experience beyond the moment. Their original meanings can remain uncertain. Shift: experience becomes representable.

02

Language & shared memory

Oral traditionStory

Language and social learning transmit skills, warnings, identities, and collective memories. Shift: experience becomes socially transmissible.

03

Writing & external memory

RecordsArchives

Writing supports records, administration, law, literature, and communication across generations. Literacy and access remain unequal. Shift: memory becomes durable outside the individual.

04

Reproducible knowledge

PrintingPublic debate

Printing expands the scale and consistency of textual reproduction, helping ideas circulate across wider networks. Shift: knowledge becomes reproducible at scale.

05

Knowledge becomes material power

ScienceIndustry

Measurements, models, technical drawings, and standardized procedures shape machinery and infrastructure. Shift: representations become material interventions.

06

Networked symbolic reality

ComputingInternet

Texts, images, transactions, and measurements become digitally searchable, copyable, and computable. Shift: symbolic information becomes networked.

07

Generative transformation

AISynthesis

AI systems learn patterns in data and generate, transform, classify, or compare representations. They can help explore possibilities, but cannot guarantee their truth. Shift: symbols become a computational possibility space.

04 / The mechanism

Compression.
Decompression.
Fixation.

The theory's central mechanism is a recursive cycle. Each stage enables the next, but each stage can also introduce loss, distortion, or power imbalance.

OPERATION 01

Compression

Complex experience is selected, abstracted, encoded, and transmitted as symbols. This makes information manageable and cumulative.

Risk: omission, distortion, loss of context, and the exclusion of undocumented experience.

OPERATION 02

Decompression

Representations are reinterpreted, compared, recombined, and expanded into hypotheses, explanations, designs, and scenarios.

Risk: plausible invention, false connections, and many variations on one inherited assumption.

OPERATION 03

Fixation

Selected interpretations become standards, policies, practices, narratives, technologies, or accepted knowledge.

Risk: institutional inertia, dogma, coercion, and immunity to correction.

Non-negotiable distinction: AI can expand the hypothesis space. It cannot make every generated hypothesis true.
05 / Human cognition

Past, present,
possible future.

Memory, perception, dreams, imagination, and prediction connect humans to different dimensions of experience. Civilization grows when these capacities are externalized, shared, criticized, and accumulated.

◷

Memory

Reconstructs the past and supports continuity. Human memory is fallible; archives preserve evidence but reflect decisions about what to collect and retain.

◌

Perception

Provides situated contact with the present. Attention, instruments, prior expectations, and context shape what becomes observable.

✳

Imagination

Creates alternatives: a bridge before it is built, a political system before it exists, or a cinematic world that never existed physically.

↗

Prediction

Anticipates consequences using patterns and models. Forecasts are uncertain and may fail when conditions or human behavior change.

Memory reconstructs the past. Perception informs the present. Imagination explores alternatives. Prediction anticipates consequences. Action tests what those representations are worth.

07 / The paradox

Why worlds
become fixed.

Civilization needs stability to coordinate action. But stability becomes dangerous when a model, narrative, or institution can no longer be challenged by evidence or legitimate dissent.

DYNAMIC SYSTEM

Open symbolic world

Evidence can revise claims. Different interpretations can be examined. Uncertainty is visible. Archives preserve contradictions. Institutions maintain correction procedures.

LearningPluralityRevision
CLOSED SYSTEM

Fixed symbolic world

One narrative dominates. Authority determines acceptable truth. Contrary evidence is dismissed or suppressed. Institutional survival becomes more important than correction.

DogmaConformityControl

Stability, truth, legitimacy, and power are not the same

08 / Philosophical metaphor

The dream of a
completed world.

“Heaven” is used here as a metaphor for the imagined endpoint at which uncertainty disappears and a symbolic order becomes complete—not as a claim about the meaning of every religious tradition.

The promise of finality

Humans imagine a condition in which injustice ends, suffering is overcome, prosperity becomes permanent, or every question has a final answer. These aspirations differ in content, but they can share a desire to overcome uncertainty and incompleteness.

The risk of symbolic closure

A model built to explain the world can become a system that decides what may count as an explanation. When contrary evidence is treated as illegitimate by definition, the model becomes resistant to learning.

The technological danger is not simply that AI may give wrong answers. It is that people may institutionalize its answers as unquestionable.

The alternative is not permanent disorder. It is an open-ended civilization: stable enough to act, transparent enough to inspect, and revisable enough to learn.

09 / Power & political economy

Who controls the
possibility engine?

Symbolic systems are shaped by ownership, access, language, institutions, and incentives. AI may democratize the production of content while concentrating the infrastructure that ranks, distributes, and monetizes it.

Preservation power

Who can record experience, preserve archives, digitize material, and determine what survives? Documentary visibility can be mistaken for historical importance.

Interpretive power

Who has the resources to analyze data, publish findings, establish standards, and challenge authoritative accounts? Some communities remain underrepresented in digital records.

Distribution power

Who owns models, compute, platforms, and ranking systems? Wider access to generation does not automatically create shared governance or accountability.

Two kinds of democratization: Generative democratization lets more people create symbolic material. Institutional democratization lets more people influence how that material is stored, evaluated, distributed, and used. The first does not automatically produce the second.

Seven principles for an open symbolic civilization

01 — Traceability

Connect important claims to inspectable sources and distinguish evidence from generated interpretation.

02 — Plurality

Allow different perspectives to be examined without treating every claim as equally supported.

03 — Contestability

Enable people to challenge consequential decisions and request correction.

04 — Revisability

Maintain procedures for updating models, archives, classifications, and policies.

05 — Distributed access

Reduce unnecessary exclusion by wealth, language, geography, or institutional gatekeeping.

06 — Accountability

Keep people and organizations answerable for systems they deploy and decisions they make.

07 — Real-world consequences

Evaluate effects on people, communities, and ecosystems—not only computational objectives.

10 / A living-delta application

When physical
change meets
symbolic power.

Bangladesh's delta offers a compelling case for connecting ecological processes, lived experience, archives, institutions, and competing historical narratives—without confusing one kind of evidence with another.

LAYER 01

Physical delta

Rivers, sediment, flooding, erosion, monsoon patterns, and ecological change.

LAYER 02

Lived delta

Livelihoods, family memory, displacement, adaptation, and community relationships.

LAYER 03

Documented delta

Land records, maps, oral histories, census data, legal claims, and research archives.

LAYER 04

Institutional delta

Government, markets, infrastructure, political representation, and development policy.

An AI-assisted archive could connect satellite imagery, historical maps, oral histories, land records, economic data, and policy documents. Each source contributes a different kind of evidence. The system should show provenance, corroboration, contradiction, uncertainty, and unresolved questions rather than manufacturing a single seamless story.

A living archive does not replace one unquestionable narrative with another. It makes the evidence behind narratives inspectable.
11 / Research program

From philosophy
to testable inquiry.

The framework becomes research when its terms are defined, its claims are operationalized, and its propositions are allowed to be challenged by evidence.

RQ 01

What does compression preserve?

Compare source material with summaries, records, and later reconstructions. Measure loss of context, accuracy, and retrievability.

RQ 02

Does AI increase interpretive diversity?

Compare human-only and AI-assisted analysis. Evaluate genuinely distinct hypotheses, source diversity, and hidden shared assumptions.

RQ 03

Does decompression improve truth-seeking?

Test whether generated alternatives improve explanations or mainly increase unsupported claims. Audit citations and reproducibility.

RQ 04

How do interpretations become authoritative?

Trace how findings, narratives, and rules become embedded in institutions, curricula, policies, and public discourse.

RQ 05

Does AI distribute or concentrate power?

Study ownership, language coverage, access, platform dependence, and the ability to challenge outputs.

RQ 06

Can institutions remain revisable?

Compare systems that preserve stability while enabling appeal, correction, dissent, and lawful change.

12 / Synthesis

Eight propositions.
One open question.

The framework links cumulative culture, symbolic representation, AI-assisted generation, institutional power, and the ethics of revision.

01

Civilization is cumulative

Representations allow experience to be preserved, transmitted, and reused across generations.

02

Compression is incomplete

Every representation selects, abstracts, and omits; no model should be confused with the whole it represents.

03

Decompression expands possibilities

Interpretation and recombination can expose new explanations, connections, and futures.

04

AI changes the scale

AI can transform large bodies of symbolic material, but remains limited by data, resources, and evaluation.

05

Possibility needs validation

Generation alone does not establish truth, feasibility, or ethical legitimacy.

06

Fixation can close inquiry

Stable institutions are necessary, but dangerous when they become immune to challenge.

07

Symbolic power has material effects

Control over archives, models, and institutions shapes which experiences and interpretations influence action.

08

Correction must be institutionalized

A mature civilization preserves knowledge while maintaining meaningful procedures for revision.

The central research question

Will AI expand humanity's ability to question its symbolic worlds—or help powerful institutions make one symbolic world harder to question?

The future depends not only on what AI can generate, but on who controls generation, evaluation, selection, and institutionalization—and on the evidence, ethics, and accountability that govern each stage.

13 / Manifesto

Beyond the
fixed world.

An open symbolic civilization is not a civilization without standards. It is one whose standards remain answerable to evidence, purpose, and the people affected by them.

Human beings inherit a world they did not create, but they do not encounter it without interpretation. Through experience, memory, imagination, language, and collective action, they build symbolic systems that allow civilization to accumulate knowledge across generations.

These systems preserve reality while transforming it. They make the world understandable, but they also influence what becomes visible, memorable, legitimate, and possible.

Artificial intelligence introduces a new capacity into this historical process. It can transform accumulated symbolic material into new combinations, explanations, representations, and scenarios. It can help examine assumptions, compare perspectives, recover overlooked relationships, and explore futures that would otherwise remain difficult to formulate.

Yet the expansion of possibility does not guarantee truth or freedom. Every system that generates possibilities also participates in selection. Evidence, institutions, incentives, and power determine which possibilities survive, which are rejected, and which become the architecture of collective life.

The opportunity is to build a civilization that accumulates knowledge without mistaking its archives for completeness, uses AI without surrendering judgment, preserves institutions without making them immutable, and creates shared realities without eliminating the right to question them.

The purpose of AI should not be to finalize reality on humanity's behalf. It should be to expand humanity's capacity to understand, test, and responsibly transform the world.
